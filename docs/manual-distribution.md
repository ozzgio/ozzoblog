# A small manual distribution routine

Start with one project for two weeks. Each project can serve a different audience; choosing one experiment at a time helps you learn which message and channel work for it. The aim here is to meet a few new people and watch them use something useful.

## Before the first post

1. Pick one specific audience and one task the tool helps them finish. For Synergym that might be a trainer assigning a workout; a homelab project speaks to people running their own infrastructure. Avoid treating every project as a developer tool.
2. Make the try-it link public and explain what someone can do with it. Call something free only when it is actually free. Private infrastructure projects are case studies, rather than tools strangers can try.
3. Put that one link and a clear description in your X profile/pinned post and LinkedIn Featured section. The blog's social links now make it easy for visitors to find your accounts.

## The weekly routine to try

| When | Manual work |
| --- | --- |
| Most weekdays, 10 minutes | Read posts by people in the selected audience. Leave two useful, specific replies. Write down one question or workaround you notice. |
| Monday | Post a short X demo: who it helps, the task it completes, a screenshot or short recording, and a try-it link. |
| Wednesday | Post one decision, limitation, or small improvement from the same project. Ask a concrete question about the workflow. |
| Friday | Share what someone tried or what you learned. If nobody tried it yet, say that and ask about one specific task. |
| Once a week | Turn the strongest example into one LinkedIn post: the problem, the result, one lesson, and a question. Reuse the screenshot; adapt the explanation. |

Reply to people who respond. Start with public conversations if reaching out feels uncomfortable. Share a link when it answers the question being discussed.

X's community guidance emphasizes replies and one-to-one conversation. LinkedIn's sharing guide recommends consistent posts, useful observations, questions, and responses to other people's posts. The cadence above is a small experiment you can adjust to your available time. [X community management](https://business.x.com/en/basics/community-management), [LinkedIn sharing guide](https://content.linkedin.com/content/dam/help/linkedin/en-us/LinkedIn-Sharing-Guide.pdf)

## Ask for a small try, then listen

A concrete invitation after a relevant conversation:

> You mentioned [task]. I made a small tool that helps with [specific step]. Would you try that step and tell me where it gets confusing?

Ask about what actually happened:

- What were you trying to get done?
- How did you do it before?
- Where did you hesitate or leave?
- Did you use it again or send it to someone?

Change the next version based on what you observe. Keep the first request small enough for someone to complete in a few minutes.

## Keep a tiny record

A note or spreadsheet is enough:

| Date | Project/audience | Conversation | Tried the tool? | Finished the task? | Follow-up or change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

For the first two weeks, aim to learn from three conversations and three real attempts at the task. Record views and likes if useful, but judge the experiment by the attempts, questions, and repeat use. Reaching those numbers is a learning target, not a reach guarantee.

At the end, decide whether to improve the same tool/message or try another project's audience. Keep the technical work tied to something you learned from a person.
